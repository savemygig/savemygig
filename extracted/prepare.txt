NEVER AGAIN
 Most DJ disasters are not bad luck. They are a missing system.
 Professionals do not rely on memory or on the night going well, they rely
 on preparation. These are the habits that stop problems before they ever
 reach the booth.
The professional rules
Non-negotiable, in the order the night actually happens.
Your music
01
Two identical drives, every gig.
The same export on both, made the same night from the same playlists. One in the bag, one in your pocket.
Why it mattersOne drive is convenience. Two drives are insurance.
02
Format for the whole room.
FAT32 with an MBR partition scheme is read by every Pioneer DJ and AlphaTheta player ever made. exFAT is read only by newer units, and NTFS by none of them.
Why it mattersYou are not formatting for your player. You are formatting for a booth you have never seen.
03
Buy drives that earn their place.
A no-name stick is where most of these stories begin. Carry drives with a reputation among working DJs. The drives worth carrying.
Why it mattersThe cheapest part of your setup carries the only thing the night cannot replace.
04
Export fresh, every time.
Re-export tonight's playlists after any library change, and always export from rekordbox. Never drag audio files onto the drive by hand.
Why it mattersAn old export is a corrupt export waiting to happen. A dragged folder has no database at all.
05
Eject like it matters.
Eject from rekordbox and wait for the computer to release the drive. On a player, press USB STOP and let the light settle before the drive moves.
Why it mattersInterrupting a write is the fastest way to corrupt the database you just spent an hour building.
06
Back up the library, not just the drive.
In rekordbox: File > Library > Backup Library, monthly, onto a different drive or the cloud. Your cues, grids and playlists live in the library, not on the stick.
Why it mattersA USB is a working copy. If it is your only copy, you do not have a backup.
Your night
07
Gig day is a freeze.
No rekordbox update, no player firmware, no operating system update on the day you play or the days around it. Update the week after a gig, never the day before one.
Why it mattersA new version can bring a new problem, and the booth is where you would meet it. Player firmware has been withdrawn by its own manufacturer after playlists stopped appearing for DJs.
08
Test it before you leave.
Load a track from the drive on a player if you own one. If you do not, reopen the export in rekordbox and confirm the device and the playlists are really there.
Why it mattersTen seconds at home costs nothing. The same discovery in the booth costs the set.
09
Know the booth before you walk in.
Ask the promoter which players and mixer are installed, and whether they are linked. Then pack for that room: the export a CDJ-3000X reads is not the one a CDJ-2000NXS2 reads. Which player reads which.
Why it mattersCompatibility is a question you answer at home, with time, or in the booth, without it.
10
Learn before you need it.
Read one thing while nothing is wrong: an error code, a compatibility quirk, a rescue move. The knowledge base and the FAQ are built for the quiet afternoon, not only for the emergency.
Why it mattersNobody learns under pressure. They remember. Familiarity is what turns a disaster into an inconvenience.
 Professionals do not just prepare their gear. They prepare their minds.
