I'VE WATCHED GREAT DJs LOSE NIGHTS
THEY DIDN'T HAVE TO LOSE.
Thirty-five years inside electronic music, from local booths to international festival management, teach you one thing above all: the night doesn't care about your preparation until the moment it tests it. And the test is almost always the same. A USB stick. A player that won't read it. A clock that won't stop.
I've stood next to DJs at the biggest moments of their careers while their drive threw E-8302 at them. The ones who survived weren't luckier, they knew the sequence. Reseat, second slot, second player, format math, survival mode. Calm, in order, no wasted seconds. The ones who didn't know it lost sets they had prepared for months.
Save My Gig is that sequence, written down. All of it. The critical path for when you play in an hour, the proper repair for when you have three, the deep rebuild for the morning after, plus the prevention system that makes all of it unnecessary. It's free, it needs no account, and inside the emergency flow nothing is sold to you. That's not generosity, it's design: a panicking DJ deserves a clear road, not a shop.
Independent, and staying that way
No manufacturer pays for placement here and no brand edits our advice. When this site says FAT32 and two identical drives, it's because booths taught us that, not because a marketing department did. Everything is checked against official documentation, and where the official answer doesn't exist, we say so instead of guessing.
And it stopped being a one-person project. What started as one DJ writing the sequence down is now a small crew of DJs and technicians who've been there, testing every step against real gear and real nights before it ships.
Why free?
Because the DJ community gave me a career, and because trust is the only currency that matters in an emergency. If this site saves your night, there's a coffee button, after you're safe, never before. Some gear links may earn a small commission; they never change what you pay and never appear inside the rescue flow.
The mission in one line
No DJ should ever lose a gig to a €15 piece of plastic.
