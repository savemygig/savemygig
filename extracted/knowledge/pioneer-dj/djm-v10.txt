DJM-V10
DJM-V10, the field manual
Reviewed: 26 July 2026
×
Booth briefing. Not a bigger 900: a different desk.
 6 channels, 4-band EQ and a
 compressor on every channel, dual headphone outputs, booth EQ,
 and an expanded send/return that combines external and built-in FX. The
 trap for DJs arriving from 3-band desks is muscle memory: the EQ you
 reach for is not where your hands think it is, and there are two more
 channel strips than your eyes expect. Walk the desk before doors, not
 during your first blend.

 Emergency fixes
A channel has no signal
Short answer. Same order as any desk, fader, assign,
 input selector, with two V10-specific suspects: the send/return routing
 and, on very old firmware, a documented bug where audio would sometimes
 fail to input to a channel, fixed in firmware 1.04.
Why this happens. Six channels means six input
 selectors that can each sit on the wrong source, and the send/return can
 detour a channel through external gear that is not returning signal.
Fix it now. Fader up, input selector matched, send/
 return disengaged for your channel, then move the source to another
 channel to isolate. If a specific channel drops audio repeatedly on an
 old unit, check the firmware version: 1.04 was the
 documented cure for exactly that.
Avoid it next time. A six-channel routing pass before
 doors takes ninety seconds and removes every one of these mid-set.

Audio cutting out with CDJ-3000s on Link
Short answer. A documented issue: potential audio
 cut-out when the V10 was connected to CDJ-3000s over the Pro DJ Link
 network, fixed in firmware 1.12.
Why this happens. V10 plus CDJ-3000s is the standard
 pairing for this desk, which is exactly why the bug mattered enough to
 document and fix.
Fix it now. If audio drops while Link is up, the
 immediate stabilizer is pulling the Link cable from the mixer, players
 keep playing audio without the network. Then get the desk to
 1.12 or later after the night.
Avoid it next time. Check the firmware version on any
 V10 booth running CDJ-3000s; this fix shipped years ago, but rental and
 venue units miss updates for years.

A USB drive the mixer refuses to see
Short answer. UASP-compatible drives, most current
 quality sticks, were not recognized until firmware 1.16.
Why this happens. Same documented bug as on the
 DJM-A9, fixed the same day, and rental desks are routinely behind.
Fix it now. Use an older or plain stick for the
 mixer-side job, update the desk to 1.16+ later.
Avoid it next time. One plain non-UASP stick in the
 bag for mixer duties.

 Known issues
Documented fixes, by firmware version
Short answer. Four real entries from the official
 change history. Fixed in 1.04: audio sometimes
 failing to input to a channel. Fixed in 1.07: incorrect
 MIDI output under certain operation. Fixed in
 1.12: the potential audio cut-out with
 CDJ-3000s over Pro DJ Link. Fixed in 1.16: UASP USB drives
 not recognized.
One behaviour change, not a bug: introduced in
 1.13, power management follows the EU Ecodesign
 20-minute timing. A desk that switched itself off during a long
 changeover is following that setting.
Do this. On a V10 you control, sit at
 1.16 or later, updated between bookings, never before one.

 Features explained
The 4-band EQ, and the muscle-memory trap
Short answer. Every channel has four EQ bands instead
 of the three your hands learned on every other DJM.
Why it matters. The knob positions shift. A DJ who
 reaches for "the low" without looking grabs a different band than
 intended, live, on a big system. This is the single most common V10
 mistake and it is purely spatial.
When professionals use it. The split low end (low and
 low-mid) is the reason this desk exists for long-blend styles: you can
 hold two basslines apart in a way 3-band EQs cannot.
Common mistakes. Not walking the strip before doors.
 Ninety seconds of touching every knob on one channel recalibrates your
 hands for the night.

The per-channel compressor
Short answer. A one-knob compressor on every channel
 that tightens and lifts the perceived loudness of a track.
Why it matters. Sets built from different eras and
 masters arrive at wildly different loudness; the compressor evens the
 night out at the channel, before the EQ fight starts.
When professionals use it. Gently, on thin or old
 material that disappears next to modern masters.
Common mistakes. Using it as a loudness war button.
 Compressing everything hard flattens the dynamics the big system was
 hired to reproduce, and inherited compressor positions from the previous
 DJ count as routing state: check them at takeover.

Six channels, in practice
Short answer. Four players plus hardware, a hybrid
 live rig, or three DJs sharing a desk without repatching, that is what
 the two extra channels buy.
Why it matters. The V10 booth is usually a busier
 booth: more sources, more cables, more people, which is why routing
 discipline matters more here than on any 4-channel desk.
When professionals use it. Hybrid sets (players plus
 drum machine plus synth) and festival stages with overlapping
 changeovers.
Common mistakes. Assuming the channel layout of the
 previous act. Six channels means six chances the input selector is not
 where you expect.

 Professional tips
Walk the desk before doors
Short answer. Ninety seconds, one channel strip, top
 to bottom, before the room fills: trim, four EQ bands, compressor,
 filter, fader, assign, send/return.
Why it matters. Every V10 horror story we hear starts
 with hands trained on a 3-band desk meeting this layout for the first
 time mid-set. The desk is not harder; it is different, and different is
 what kills you at 2am.
Do this. Make the walk part of your soundcheck
 ritual, and repeat the takeover glance at every handover: on this desk,
 inherited state includes compressor and send/return positions.

 Preventive maintenance
Keeping the desk reliable
Short answer. Connector care, covered transport, and
 firmware current between bookings, the same trio as every desk, with
 more connectors to care about.
Why it matters. Six channels of inputs plus
 send/return means roughly double the cable connections of a 900-class
 desk, and every one is a future intermittent fault if strained.
Do this. Unplug by the connector, inspect before
 packing, keep it covered, and hold firmware at 1.16+.

 Compatibility
Players, software and Pro DJ Link
Short answer. Pro DJ Link across player generations,
 with the CDJ-3000 pairing being the standard, and the one with a
 documented, fixed audio cut-out (firmware
 1.12). Library format on your USB is a
 player-side question and never touches the mixer.
Do this. On a V10-plus-3000s booth, one glance at the
 mixer firmware answers whether the known Link issue is even possible.
