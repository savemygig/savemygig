DJM-REC
DJM-REC, on the record
Reviewed: 28 July 2026
×
Booth briefing. DJM-REC is AlphaTheta's iPhone and iPad
 app that records and live streams your set through one USB cable into
 a compatible DJM mixer, writing a time-stamped track list while you
 play. It sits entirely outside your playback chain: a failed recording costs
 you a file, never a set. The things that do go wrong at gigs are venue mixer
 firmware and platform quirks, and both are listed below.

 Understanding DJM-REC
What DJM-REC actually is
Short answer. AlphaTheta's recording and live-streaming
 app for iPhone and iPad. One USB cable between the device and a compatible
 DJM mixer carries the digital audio, so there is no audio interface, no
 cable loom and no gain guesswork in the booth.
What it does, per AlphaTheta. High-quality recording and
 live streaming that minimizes clipping and distortion, live streams that can
 carry the track title and artist name, automatic time-stamped track lists as
 you play, and simple remastering and sharing of the finished mix.
What it is not. It is not a player, not a library tool
 and not part of the rekordbox export chain. Nothing it does touches the USB
 drive you play from. If the app crashes mid-set, your set does not.

 Compatibility
Which mixers officially support DJM-REC
Short answer. AlphaTheta's official compatibility list
 names DJM-V10, DJM-V10-LF, DJM-TOUR1, DJM-V5, DJM-A9, DJM-900NXS2, DJM-750MK2 and DJM-450.
 If the booth has a digital send/return DJM from that list, DJM-REC works
 over one USB cable.
The caveat that matters. Not every mixer on the list
 supports every feature. Track and artist names on the stream, and track
 metadata saved into the recording, work on a subset of these mixers, and
 AlphaTheta documents that per model. Some all-in-one systems are also
 supported, but AlphaTheta does not publish one flat list of them on the
 pages we checked, so we are not inventing one. Check the
 official compatibility article
 for your exact hardware before you rely on it.
Avoid it next time. Compatibility is a home question,
 not a booth question. Confirm your mixer and your feature set before the
 gig, not while the opener is playing.

 Live streaming
Which streaming services work
Short answer. Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Live, Instagram, Snapchat, Mixcloud Live, Restream are supported
 directly, and a Custom option takes a manual stream URL and key for anything
 else that speaks standard streaming protocols.
One account trap, officially documented. Restream
 accounts created through Facebook integration cannot log in to DJM-REC. If
 your Restream login is "Continue with Facebook", sort that out at home.

Track titles on the stream (Now Playing)
Short answer. The Now Playing display, track title and
 artist name on the stream, works on Twitch, YouTube, Facebook Live, Mixcloud Live and
 Restream. It is not available on Instagram or Snapchat.
Why this matters. Whether Now Playing works depends on
 three things at once: the platform (above), the mixer model (see
 compatibility), and an officially acknowledged issue
 where titles sometimes fail to display at all. Treat it as a bonus for the
 viewers, never as your set documentation.

 Known issues
Recording halts mid-set on venue mixers
Short answer. On the DJM-900NXS2, the booth-standard
 mixer of the last decade, recording halts were fixed in mixer firmware
 2.06 and again in 2.08 for iOS and
 iPadOS audio. A venue unit on older firmware can still stop your recording
 cold, and it will pick the best moment of the set to do it.
Fix it now. There is nothing to fix in the app. Check
 the mixer's firmware version in UTILITY at soundcheck; if it predates the
 fixes, record anyway but expect gaps, or record a backup on a second device
 off the REC OUT.
Avoid it next time. Treat venue gear as out of date
 until proven otherwise. The same rule that protects your USB exports
 protects your recordings.

Track info missing from the stream or the file
Short answer. Two officially acknowledged issues: the
 title or artist of the playing track sometimes is not displayed on the live
 stream, and track information sometimes is not saved into the recording
 file.
What to do about it. Use the auto track list as a first
 draft and verify it against your set after the gig, while you still remember
 the transitions. If the track list is contractually required, radio mixes
 and some promoters do require it, keep your own record as well.

All-in-one systems: connect first, then record
Short answer. Two officially acknowledged issues with
 compatible all-in-one systems: an error can prevent the live stream from
 starting, and connecting an all-in-one while a recording is running can stop
 that recording.
Avoid it next time. Order of operations: connect the
 hardware, confirm the app sees it, then start the recording or stream. Never
 replug mid-recording.

 Best practices
Never debut the combination live
Short answer. The single most repeated official fix
 across AlphaTheta's mixers is "update the firmware", and DJM-REC breakage
 has twice been on the receiving end of that on the DJM-900NXS2 alone. The
 rule that follows: never debut an OS, app and firmware combination at a
 gig.
Do this. Update the app, the phone OS and your own
 mixer's firmware at home, then test the exact chain you will use: this
 phone, this cable, this mixer, this platform. At the venue, check the
 mixer firmware at soundcheck and start the stream before doors if you can.
 And the recording is a recording, not a backup: nothing in DJM-REC replaces
 the USB rules that get your music to play in the first place.

Before you erase anything, read this
Formatting and erasing permanently destroys data. Once a
 drive is formatted, the music on it is gone unless you have another copy
 or pay for professional recovery. There is no undo.
Copy the files off first whenever there is time. If you
 can plug the drive into a computer and the files are visible, copy them
 somewhere safe before you format anything.
Make sure you are erasing the right drive. Formatting the
 wrong device, an external backup, a second USB, your system disk, is the
 single most expensive mistake in this whole process.
This is general guidance, not a guarantee. Every drive,
 player, firmware version and library is different. We cannot see your
 setup and we cannot promise any step will work for you.
