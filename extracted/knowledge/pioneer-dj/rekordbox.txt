Booth briefing. Format the drive FAT32 with an MBR

 That combination is readable by every Pioneer and AlphaTheta player in this

 Emergency fixes
USB mounts on the CDJ but shows no playlists
Short answer. Nine times out of ten in 2026, this is not a
 broken drive. It is the wrong database format, and it is the
 most confusing failure in modern DJing because the player gives you
 no error code at all. The stick mounts. The player is happy. Your
 playlists are simply not there.
Why this happens. rekordbox can write two different
 databases onto a USB drive: Device Library, the long-standing
 format, and OneLibrary, the newer one. A player reads one or
 the other. If your drive carries only Device Library and you plug it into a
 CDJ-3000X, the playlists will not appear. See what these
 two formats actually are if you want the full picture.
Your music is not gone. The audio files on the drive are
 never deleted by this. It is only the playlist database the player cannot
 see. In the FOLDER or browse view the tracks are usually still there,
 which is exactly what saves a set while you sort the rest out later.
Fix it now.
At the gig, switch to FOLDER view. Your audio files are still on the drive. You lose cues, grids and playlists, but you keep playing. That is the whole point of the rescue.
Get to rekordbox 7.2.13 or later. From this version rekordbox can write both database formats onto the same drive, so one stick works in every booth.
Re-export your playlists to the drive. Export, do not drag and drop. Dragging audio files gives you no database at all.
Eject properly, from rekordbox. Pulling a drive mid-write is the single most common way to corrupt the database you just built.
Test it before you leave the house if you own a player, or arrive early enough to test on the club's.
Avoid it next time. Keep rekordbox at 7.2.13
 or later so every export carries both database formats, and never drag audio
 files onto the drive by hand, only export.

What do the CDJ error codes mean?
Short answer. Two error codes cover almost every rekordbox-related
 stop on a CDJ-3000 or CDJ-3000X. E-8302 means the
 player cannot read the track data at all; E-8304 and
 E-8305 mean the file format itself is unsupported.
These are the official definitions for the CDJ-3000 and CDJ-3000X. Older CDJs (CDJ-350 and CDJ-850 era) can map some codes differently.
E-8302CANNOT PLAY TRACK. Officially: the track data on the storage device cannot be read correctly. Usually a corrupt export or a file system the player does not accept. Full fix →E-8304 / E-8305UNSUPPORTED FILE FORMAT. Officially: music files that cannot be played by the unit have been loaded. Re-encode the offending tracks, or re-export from rekordbox.
Avoid it next time. Export FAT32 + MBR, keep rekordbox at
 7.2.13 or later, and eject from rekordbox every time, the
 three habits that make these codes rare instead of routine.
 We are not publishing a definition for E-8306. It does not appear on the
 official error pages and the older manuals contradict each other. If
 you have hit it, tell us what the screen said and what
 fixed it, and we will document it properly.

rekordbox export failed, or the player shows nothing
Short answer. A failed or corrupt export is a database
 problem, not a music problem. Your tracks are almost always fine, the index
 the CDJ reads is broken. A clean re-export fixes most cases.
Why this happens. Either the export itself never completed
 (a failing drive, a full drive, or a locked file), or it "finished" but landed
 corrupt, classic after pulling the drive without ejecting it properly.
Fix it now.
Close rekordbox, unplug and replug the USB, reopen rekordbox in EXPORT mode.
Check free space. An export onto a nearly-full drive corrupts silently.
If the device shows in rekordbox: delete tonight's playlists from the device, re-sync them fresh, and eject with the eject button.
If the device errors or will not sync: copy any music you need off the drive, format it FAT32 + MBR, and export again to the clean drive.
Avoid it next time. Export tonight's playlists only, not
 the whole library, a large export is where corruption tends to live, and
 always eject from rekordbox, then wait for the OS to release the drive.

rekordbox is not detecting my USB at all
Short answer. If the drive is missing from the Devices
 list, the cause is almost always one of three things, in this order: you are
 not in Export mode, the operating system itself cannot see the drive, or the
 drive is formatted in a way rekordbox cannot write to.
Why this happens. The device tree only appears in
 EXPORT mode, not Performance or Lighting. USB hubs
 and dongles are the single most common reason a drive mounts on the OS but
 never reaches rekordbox. And rekordbox exports to FAT32 or exFAT only, a
 drive formatted NTFS or APFS may mount on the computer but refuse the export.
Fix it now.
Confirm you are in EXPORT mode, top left of rekordbox.
Check the computer sees the drive first, in Finder or File Explorer. If it is not there either, this is a drive or connection problem, not rekordbox.
Plug in direct, no hub, straight into the computer.
Restart rekordbox with the drive already inserted. Some versions only scan removable drives at launch.
Avoid it next time. Keep the drive FAT32 + MBR, skip hubs
 when exporting, and if the drive mounts and disappears on its own, treat that
 as a failing drive and retire it, not a rekordbox bug.

 Understanding rekordbox
What are OneLibrary and Device Library?
Short answer. Both are the index rekordbox writes to your
 drive so a player can show your playlists, cues, grids and artwork without a
 computer. They are two different formats, and a player reads one or the other.
Why this exists. OneLibrary was introduced in
 rekordbox 6.6.11 (March 2023),
 originally for the OPUS-QUAD, and it is the format OneLibrary players expect
 (CDJ-3000X, CDJ-1500X, OPUS-QUAD, OMNIS-DUO, XDJ-AZ, XDJ-AN). Device Library
 is what everything before it reads.
The trap is that the split does not follow the age of the gear the way you
 would expect. The CDJ-3000 is a Device Library player. The CDJ-3000X
 is a OneLibrary player. Two decks with nearly the same name, in the
 same booth, reading different formats.
Avoid the trap. Stop trying to remember which player reads
 which. Keep rekordbox at 7.2.13 or later and every export
 carries both formats on the same drive, so it stops mattering.

How rekordbox exports actually work
Short answer. A CDJ reads a real database, cues, grids,
 playlists, that rekordbox writes only when you use the Export panel. Dragging
 audio files onto the drive by hand does not create one.
Why this matters. CDJs can still play plain audio files
 from a FAT32 drive with no database at all, in the FOLDER view, that is the
 core of our critical-path rescue. But Sync, Quantize, hot cues, grids and
 playlists all come from the database, and the only way to write it is Export.
Do this. Build your playlists in rekordbox, select the
 device in the left-hand tree, then export (drag the playlists onto the device,
 or use the Export panel). Eject from rekordbox, not the operating system, once
 it finishes.
Avoid it next time. Re-export after every library change.
 An old export is a corrupt export waiting to happen, because everything you
 changed since the last export exists only in rekordbox until you export again.

 USB & library preparation
Is FAT32 really limited to 32GB?
Short answer. No, and this myth costs DJs money. FAT32
 supports volumes up to 2TB. The 32GB ceiling you have run into
 is a limit in Windows' own formatting dialog, not in the file system.
Why the myth exists. Windows will not let you format a
 drive larger than 32GB as FAT32 through its normal Format dialog, so people
 assume the file system itself has that ceiling. It does not.
Fix it now. On Windows you can format a larger drive to
 FAT32 with a free tool such as fat32format or its graphical
 version guiformat (both from Ridgecrop Consultants). On a Mac,
 Disk Utility does it natively: choose MS-DOS (FAT)
 with a Master Boot Record scheme.
Avoid it next time. The real FAT32 limit is
 4GB per file. A 320kbps MP3 is around 9MB and even a long
 uncompressed WAV rarely passes 1GB, so for a music library it never matters.
Related: Full formatting walkthroughMBR vs GPT
MBR or GPT: does the partition scheme matter?
Short answer. Use MBR (Master Boot Record).
 AlphaTheta's own rekordbox manual documents file systems and says nothing at
 all about partition schemes, so this is our recommendation, not a published
 requirement, but we have seen the alternative fail too often to stay quiet.
Why we recommend it. A GPT drive is a repeat offender in
 the "formatted fine, player refuses it" category, and MBR costs you nothing
 in return.
Do this. On Mac: Disk Utility → Erase →
 MS-DOS (FAT) + Master Boot Record.
 On Windows, the standard Format dialog defaults to MBR for drives under 2TB, so
 most people already have this right without knowing it.
Avoid it next time. Do not let a formatting tool default
 you into GPT because it looked like the modern option. For a DJ USB, it is not.

Why do some USB drives work better than others?
Short answer. Cheap, no-name sticks are how most
 "random" USB failures at gigs actually start.
Why this happens. A CDJ reads a drive far harder than a
 laptop ever does. Budget drives fail under that sustained read pattern more
 often than quality ones, and a struggling drive often gives you a warning
 first: tracks load fine but waveforms crawl or browsing lags.
Fix it now. If a drive is already showing that warning,
 do not trust it for the next gig. Copy your music off and retire it.
Avoid it next time. Buy a quality metal-bodied 32-64GB
 drive from a brand you have heard of, and carry two identical drives with the
 same export on both. A second drive is the only backup that works when you
 are already standing in the booth.

 documents file systems and says nothing at all about partition
 schemes, see MBR vs GPT for why we still
 recommend MBR.

The rekordbox version numbers worth knowing
Short answer. Two numbers matter more than any other
 rekordbox fact: never use 7.2.12, and get to
 7.2.13 or later.
Why this matters. 7.2.12 was withdrawn by
 AlphaTheta because tracks exported with that version could fail to load on
 their own hardware. It was fixed in 7.2.13, which is also
 the first version that writes both Device Library and OneLibrary to the same
 drive. OneLibrary itself was introduced in 6.6.11.
Do this. Check your installed version (Preferences or
 About, inside rekordbox) before a big show, and if you are on
 7.2.12, update before you export anything.
Avoid it next time. Converting a library format is
 one-way. AlphaTheta's own wording: if you convert when OneLibrary already
 exists on the drive, it is overwritten, and any playlists or playback
 histories stored only in OneLibrary are lost. Convert onto a fresh drive when
 you can, and never update rekordbox or firmware on gig day itself, a fresh
 version can bring a fresh problem and the booth is where you meet it.

 Best practices
The setup that never surprises you
Short answer. Five habits, done every time, make almost
 every failure on this page a non-event.
FAT32, MBR, on a 32 to 64GB drive from a brand you have heard of.
rekordbox 7.2.13 or later, exported, not dragged.
Two identical drives, same export on both, in different pockets.
Eject from rekordbox, every single time.
Test on a player before the drive ever matters.

Back up your library, not just your USB
Short answer. Back up the rekordbox library itself,
 monthly, not just the USB you export to.
Why this matters. The USB export is disposable, you can
 rebuild it from the library in minutes. The library is not disposable, it
 holds years of cues, grids and playlists that a dead laptop takes with it if
 there is no backup.
Do this. In rekordbox: File → Library → Backup
 Library, to a different drive or to cloud storage.
Avoid it next time. Do not treat the export on your gig
 USB as your only copy of anything. It is not a backup, it is a working copy.

 Tips & tricks
Re-export after every library change
Short answer. An old export is a corrupt export waiting
 to happen.
Why this happens. Every playlist edit, cue change or new
 track after your last export lives only inside rekordbox until you export
 again. The CDJ never sees any of it, and a stale export is also more likely
 to have been through an unsafe eject somewhere in its history.
Do this. Re-export tonight's playlists fresh, every time
 you play, even if you exported "recently."
Avoid it next time. Export tonight's set, not your whole
 library. A large, rarely-touched export is exactly where corruption tends to
 show up first.

Before you erase anything, read this
Formatting and erasing permanently destroys data. Once a
 drive is formatted, the music on it is gone unless you have another copy
 or pay for professional recovery. There is no undo.
Copy the files off first whenever there is time. If you
 can plug the drive into a computer and the files are visible, copy them
 somewhere safe before you format anything.
Make sure you are erasing the right drive. Formatting the
 wrong device, an external backup, a second USB, your system disk, is the
 single most expensive mistake in this whole process.
This is general guidance, not a guarantee. Every drive,
 player, firmware version and library is different. We cannot see your
 setup and we cannot promise any step will work for you.
