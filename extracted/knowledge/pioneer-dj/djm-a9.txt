DJM-A9
DJM-A9, the field manual
Reviewed: 26 July 2026
×
Booth briefing. The 4-channel successor
 to the DJM-900NXS2's spot in high-end booths: 14 Beat FX,
 two independent headphone outputs, one USB-A port plus two USB-B/USB-C for
 computers, Wi-Fi for the Stagehand monitoring app, and, worth knowing
 before you ever need it, a built-in Bluetooth audio input.
 Firmware history matters on this desk: a rare freeze under heavy Pro DJ
 Link load was fixed in 1.11, so check the version on
 gear you do not control.

 Emergency fixes
A channel has no signal
Short answer. Channel fader, crossfader assign, input
 selector, in that order, before suspecting the desk.
Why this happens. Same as on any four-channel desk:
 the fader can be down, the crossfader assign can route the channel to the
 side you are not pushing, and the input selector can sit on a source that
 is not playing. On this desk there is one extra suspect: the Multi I/O
 section's channel selector, if an insert was left engaged on your channel
 by the previous DJ, the signal is detouring through gear that may not be
 returning it.
Fix it now. Fader up, assign checked, input selector
 matched to your source, then check the Multi I/O routing is not inserted
 on your channel. Still silent: move the source to another channel to
 isolate the strip.
Avoid it next time. A full routing pass before doors,
 including Multi I/O state, not just faders and trims.

Mixer frozen mid-set
Short answer. On this desk that is not folklore: a
 rare freeze under heavy Pro DJ Link network load was documented and fixed
 in firmware 1.11.
Why this happens. Booths running full Link chains
 (players, lighting, Stagehand) push exactly the network load the fix
 addressed. A desk still on older firmware can still do it.
Fix it now. Audio that is already routed usually keeps
 passing; do not power-cycle in a panic while sound is still coming out.
 If the desk is truly locked, a restart is the only path, time it to a
 track with a long runway, and warn the floor manager first.
Avoid it next time. Firmware 1.11 or
 later on any A9 you control, and report the version to the venue when a
 rental unit freezes.

A USB drive the mixer refuses to see
Short answer. If a modern, fast USB stick is invisible
 to the mixer while older sticks work, that was a documented bug:
 UASP-compatible drives were not recognized until firmware
 1.07.
Why this happens. UASP is the faster protocol most
 current quality drives ship with, exactly the drives a professional
 carries.
Fix it now. Try an older or cheaper stick for the
 mixer-side job (recordings), and get the desk updated to
 1.07 or later after the night.
Avoid it next time. Keep one plain, non-UASP stick in
 the bag for mixer duties; it is also the stick you can lend without pain.

DVS input dead after swapping laptops
Short answer. A documented early bug: switching a
 PC/Mac between the USB ports could break the DVS phono input on USB-B.
 Fixed in firmware 1.02.
Why this happens. Back-to-back DVS sets are exactly
 the workflow that swaps computers across ports mid-night.
Fix it now. Re-select the input source for the
 channel, and if it stays dead, restart your DJ software with the cable in
 the port you intend to keep. Desk on 1.02+ does not
 do this.
Avoid it next time. Agree port assignments before a
 B2B DVS night instead of hot-swapping.

 Known issues
Documented fixes, by firmware version
Short answer. Four entries in the official change
 history matter in the real world. Fixed in 1.11: the
 rare freeze under heavy Pro DJ Link network load. Fixed in
 1.07: UASP-compatible USB drives not recognized. Fixed in
 1.02: the insert method intermittently disabling
 itself when switching the Multi I/O input selector, and the DVS phono
 input malfunction on USB-B after swapping computers across ports.
One behaviour change, not a bug: introduced in
 1.05, power management follows the EU Ecodesign
 20-minute timing. A desk that "turned itself off" between soundcheck and
 doors is following that setting; check the utility menu.
Do this. On an A9 you control, sit at
 1.11 or later, updated between bookings.

 Features explained
The Bluetooth input
Short answer. The desk accepts wireless audio from a
 phone or laptop as an input source, no cable, no adapter.
Why it matters. This site's emergency doctrine treats
 playing off a phone as the unnumbered last resort, mostly because nobody
 carries the right cable. On an A9 booth, the cable problem is gone: if
 every drive and every player has failed, a phone pairs straight into the
 desk and the room stays alive while you work the real fix.
When professionals use it. Warm-up and handover
 moments, and as the bridge that buys minutes in a genuine multi-failure
 night.
Common mistakes. Treating it as a set medium rather
 than a bridge, and forgetting to kill the phone's notification sounds
 before pairing it to a club system.

Two independent headphone outputs
Short answer. Two headphone sections with separate
 level and mix controls, so two DJs cue independently at the same time.
Why it matters. On single-output desks, B2B partners
 share one cue bus and fight over the mix knob. Here each side owns its
 own.
When professionals use it. Every back-to-back, and
 handovers where the incoming DJ pre-cues while the outgoing one still
 monitors.
Common mistakes. Diagnosing "dead headphones" on the
 output whose level knob a previous DJ zeroed; check both sections before
 blaming your cans.
Related: Cue troubleshooting on the 900NXS2
Beat FX, including the three newer ones
Short answer. 14 Beat FX types,
 including Mobius, Triplet Filter and Triplet Roll, which did not exist on
 the DJM-900NXS2.
Why it matters. Beat-synced effects land musically
 without manual tapping, and Mobius in particular builds endless-riser
 tension out of any source.
When professionals use it. Transitions and builds,
 sparingly, the same discipline as on any FX desk.
Common mistakes. Same as every DJM: leaving an effect
 engaged after the mix, and trusting a wrong BPM read, confirm it before a
 beat-synced effect goes to the room.

 Professional tips
The A9 handover routine
Short answer. The desk is built for clean handovers,
 two computer ports, two headphone sections, but only if both DJs claim
 their lane before the night, not during it.
Why it matters. The documented DVS port-swap bug
 (fixed in 1.02) happened exactly in hot-swap
 moments, and the Multi I/O insert state carries over between DJs
 silently.
Do this. Agree ports and headphone sections at
 soundcheck, and make Multi I/O state part of your takeover glance: trims,
 faders, assign, insert.
Common mistakes. Inheriting the previous DJ's insert
 routing without noticing until your channel sounds wrong.

 Preventive maintenance
Keeping the desk reliable
Short answer. Connector care over everything, and
 firmware current between bookings.
Why it matters. This desk's real documented issues
 were all firmware, fixed in updates; the hardware faults that remain are
 the universal ones, worn USB and RCA connectors from years of swaps.
Do this. Unplug by the connector, inspect cables
 before they go in the bag, keep the desk covered in transport, and hold
 firmware at 1.11+ with updates timed away from gigs.

 Compatibility
Players, software and Pro DJ Link
Short answer. Pro DJ Link with any generation of
 player, one USB-A port plus two USB-B/USB-C computer ports, Bluetooth
 audio in, Wi-Fi for Stagehand monitoring.
Why it matters. The mixer does not care which library
 format your USB carries, that is a player-side question. What it does
 care about is firmware when the Link chain is big and busy, see the
 freeze fixed in 1.11.
Do this. Confirm your DVS or software license covers
 this desk before soundcheck, and check the firmware version when walking
 into an unfamiliar A9 booth.
