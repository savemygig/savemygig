Why is my USB not detected on a Pioneer CDJ or XDJ?
The most common causes, in order: the drive is formatted as NTFS or exFAT instead of FAT32; the partition map is GPT (we recommend MBR); the Rekordbox database on the drive is corrupt (often from an unsafe eject); or the drive hardware is failing. Reseat the drive, try the second slot and the other player first, many "dead" drives are just a bad mount.

What does CDJ error E-8302 mean?
On the CDJ-3000 and CDJ-3000X, E-8302 reads "CANNOT PLAY TRACK": the player cannot read the track data on the drive, in most cases a corrupt Rekordbox export or a wrong file system. With a laptop you can usually fix it on-site: re-export fresh from Rekordbox, or, once everything on the drive that exists nowhere else is copied off, reformat FAT32/MBR and re-export. Never format a drive that still holds your only copy of anything.

How do I format a USB for CDJs?
FAT32 file system, MBR (Master Boot Record) partition scheme. On Mac: Disk Utility → Show All Devices → select the drive (not the volume) → Erase → MS-DOS (FAT) + Master Boot Record. On Windows the built-in Format dialog only offers FAT32 up to 32 GB; for a 64 GB drive use the free guiformat tool, the full walkthrough is linked below. Use a quality 32-64 GB drive.

Should I use exFAT or FAT32 for DJing?
FAT32, unless you know every player you will touch reads exFAT. FAT32 works on every Pioneer player ever made. exFAT is only read by newer units on recent firmware, so a drive that works on a CDJ-3000 can be refused by the CDJ-2000NXS2 in the next booth. FAT32 is the format that never surprises you.

Can I play tracks that were not exported with Rekordbox?
Yes. CDJs can play plain audio files from a FAT32 USB via the FOLDER view. You lose cues, grids, playlists, Sync and Quantize, but you keep the gig. That is the core of our critical-path rescue.

My export finished but the player shows nothing. Why?
The database landed corrupt, classic after pulling the drive without ejecting. Delete the playlists from the device in Rekordbox, re-sync them fresh, and eject with the Rekordbox eject button. If the device errors, copy your music off, format clean, export again.

What do I do when Rekordbox says the export failed?
Work in order: eject and re-seat the drive, check there is free space, then delete the device playlists and re-sync fresh. If the export keeps failing, the drive itself is usually the problem, copy the music off and reformat FAT32/MBR before exporting again.

Rekordbox is not detecting my USB. What now?
If the operating system sees the drive but Rekordbox does not, it is almost always the file system or the partition map, not the software. Confirm the drive is FAT32 with an MBR scheme, then restart Rekordbox with the drive already connected.

Why are waveforms not loading on my CDJ?
The audio is on the drive but the analysis data is missing or unreadable. Usually the tracks were copied manually instead of exported from Rekordbox, or the export was interrupted. Re-analyse in Rekordbox and export properly and the waveforms come back.

What is Emergency Loop mode?
When a CDJ runs out of readable audio mid-track (failing drive, corrupt file), it loops the last seconds instead of going silent. Mix out on the other player, then deal with the drive. One loop is a warning; two is a dying drive.

How many USB drives should I bring to a gig?
Two, identical, with the same export on both. A second drive is the only backup that works when you are already standing in the booth, and it turns a dead night into a ten-second swap.

What size USB drive is best for DJing?
32-64 GB is the sweet spot. Big enough for a full working library, small enough that FAT32 formats without a fight. Bigger drives are slower to format, slower to scan and no more useful for a night of music.

How do I move my Rekordbox library to a new laptop without losing cues?
Back up the library from Rekordbox itself, move the music folder with the same structure, then restore the backup on the new machine. Copying only the audio files loses every cue, grid and playlist, which is the mistake almost everyone makes once.
