exFAT vs FAT32:
WHICH ONE FOR CDJs?
The answer that never fails: FAT32 with an MBR partition map plays on every Rekordbox-compatible player ever made. exFAT works only on newer hardware. If you play in booths you don't control, FAT32 is the professional choice.
Compatibility, honestly
FAT32 + MBR, works on CDJ-850, 900, 2000, 2000NXS/NXS2, 3000, every XDJ. The universal key.
exFAT, supported on CDJ-3000, CDJ-3000X, CDJ-1500X, XDJ-RX3, XDJ-XZ and XDJ-AZ (firmware matters, see the player table); refused by every CDJ-2000, including the NXS2, still common in clubs worldwide. One trap almost nobody mentions: rekordbox writes exFAT to USB drives only. It will not export to an exFAT SD card at all, whatever the player supports.
NTFS / APFS, never. AlphaTheta's own rekordbox manual lists NTFS as unsupported on both SD cards and USB drives, so rekordbox will not export to it in the first place.
Partition scheme, choose MBR. AlphaTheta documents file systems but not partition schemes, so treat this one as our recommendation rather than a published requirement. A drive left on GPT, which is what you get if you erase the volume instead of the device on a Mac, is a recurring cause of "it formatted fine but the player refuses it". MBR costs you nothing.
"But FAT32 has a 4GB limit!"
Per file, not per drive. No DJ track approaches 4GB; even a 30-minute WAV is under 1GB. The only DJs who genuinely need exFAT are the ones carrying multi-hour uncompressed recordings on their gig drive, which is its own mistake. For music libraries, the limit is irrelevant.
The trap: how drives ship
Most drives above 32GB ship exFAT from the factory, so a brand-new "professional" 64GB stick fails on a CDJ-2000 out of the box.
Windows won't offer FAT32 for large drives in the Format dialog; on Mac, choose MS-DOS (FAT) in Disk Utility with Master Boot Record - full walkthrough here.
The rule to remember
Format for the oldest player you might ever face, not the most modern one you own. The festival main stage has CDJ-3000s; the afterparty basement has a CDJ-2000 with a cigarette burn. FAT32 plays both.
