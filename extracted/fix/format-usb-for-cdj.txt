Format a USB for CDJ
Last verified: 26 July 2026 · about 10 minutes
The answer:FAT32 file system,
 MBR partition scheme, on a 32–64 GB drive.
 Every Pioneer CDJ and XDJ ever made reads that combination.
Formatting erases the drive permanently. Copy everything off it first.
The 60-second version
Mac: Disk Utility → View → Show All Devices → select the drive, not the volume → Erase → MS-DOS (FAT) + Master Boot Record.
Windows: right-click the drive in Explorer → Format → FAT32 → Quick format is fine.
Put music on it with rekordbox 7.2.13 or later in EXPORT mode. Do not drag files.
Load a track from it on a player before the drive matters.
If something goes wrong
Open only the one that describes you.
FAT32 isn't in the list on WindowsAlmost always a drive bigger than 32GB. Three ways out.
 This is a limit in the Windows formatting dialog, not in FAT32.
 FAT32 itself supports volumes up to 2 TB. Microsoft simply
 stopped offering it above 32 GB in the graphical tool. Pick whichever of
 these suits you:
Use a free formatter.guiformat (or its command-line twin fat32format), both from Ridgecrop Consultants, will format a large drive to FAT32 in a few seconds. This is what most DJs end up using.
Windows 11 command line. Recent builds raised the built-in format command's FAT32 limit to 2 TB, even though the graphical dialog still caps at 32 GB. Open a terminal as administrator and run format X: /FS:FAT32 /Q, replacing X with your drive letter. Check the letter twice.
Shrink it. Disk Management → shrink the volume to 32 GB → format that partition FAT32. You lose the rest of the capacity, which for a gig drive rarely matters.
 The tempting alternative is exFAT, because Windows offers it right there in the
 dropdown. Do not take it unless you know every player you will touch reads exFAT.

There's no Scheme option in Mac Disk UtilityYou selected the volume instead of the drive.
 Disk Utility hides the partition scheme unless you are looking at the
 physical device. By default it only shows you the volume
 sitting on top of it.
In Disk Utility, choose View → Show All Devices.
The sidebar now shows the drive with the volume indented underneath it.
Select the top-level entry, the one with the manufacturer name, not the indented one.
Click Erase. The Scheme dropdown now appears. Choose Master Boot Record.
 This matters more than it sounds. Erasing only the volume leaves the existing
 partition map in place, which on any modern drive is GPT. The
 format reports success, the drive mounts perfectly on your Mac, and the CDJ
 refuses it. That is the single most common "but it formatted fine" story.
It formatted fine but the CDJ still won't read itFour causes, in the order worth checking.
The scheme is GPT, not MBR. The usual suspect, and invisible from the computer. Re-erase selecting the device rather than the volume (see above). On Windows check Disk Management → right-click the disk → Properties → Volumes. To be straight with you: AlphaTheta documents which file systems rekordbox writes, and says nothing about partition schemes, so MBR is our recommendation rather than a published rule. It costs nothing and it removes a variable.
It is exFAT, not FAT32. Drives above 32 GB ship exFAT from the factory, so a brand-new "professional" 64 GB stick fails on an older CDJ out of the box. If you are using an SD card rather than a USB stick, exFAT is not an option at all: rekordbox will not export to an exFAT SD card.
The music was dragged on, not exported. The player may show nothing at all in playlist view. Try the FOLDER browse button: if the tracks are there, that is what happened.
The drive itself is failing. If it formats but errors partway through copying, stop trusting it. Two identical drives, always →
How do I know it actually worked?Thirty seconds now beats finding out in the booth.
Check the format. Mac: select the drive in Disk Utility, it should read MS-DOS (FAT32) and Master Boot Record. Windows: right-click in Explorer → Properties → File system should say FAT32.
Export, then eject properly. Use rekordbox's own eject button. Pulling the drive mid-write is the most common way to corrupt the database you just built.
Load a track on a real player. Your own, or arrive early enough to test on the club's. A drive that has never been tested is not a backup, it is a hope.
What size drive should I buy?32-64 GB. Bigger is genuinely worse here.
 32–64 GB is the sweet spot: big enough for a full working library,
 small enough that FAT32 formats without a fight. Larger drives take longer to
 format, longer for a player to scan, and buy you nothing for one night of music.
 The FAT32 limit worth knowing is 4 GB per file, not per
 drive. A 320kbps MP3 is around 9 MB, and even a long uncompressed WAV
 rarely passes 1 GB, so for a music library it never comes up.

The rule worth remembering
Format for the oldest player you might ever face, not the most modern one you
 own. The festival main stage has CDJ-3000s. The afterparty basement has a
 CDJ-2000 with a cigarette burn on it. FAT32 plus MBR plays both.
