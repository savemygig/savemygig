CDJ ERROR E-8302:
WHAT IT MEANS, HOW TO FIX IT
Short version: E-8302 reads CANNOT PLAY TRACK on the CDJ-3000 and CDJ-3000X: the player cannot read the track data on your USB. Nine times out of ten it's a corrupt Rekordbox database or a wrong file system, both fixable, often before your set.
What causes it
Corrupt Rekordbox export, usually from pulling the USB without ejecting, or an interrupted sync.
Wrong file system. NTFS is never supported, by the player or by rekordbox itself. A drive left on a GPT partition map is the other common culprit. FAT32 with an MBR scheme is the combination that works in every booth (newer CDJ-3000 firmware also reads exFAT, but FAT32 never surprises you).
A failing drive, bad sectors right where the database lives.
Old player firmware meeting a new Rekordbox export format.
The immediate fix (at the venue)
Eject properly (USB STOP), reseat the drive, try the other slot and the other player.
If another player reads it, play from that deck via Pro DJ Link.
With a laptop: open the USB in Rekordbox. If Rekordbox reads it, do a fresh export of tonight's playlist. If it doesn't, copy your music files off, reformat FAT32/MBR, re-export tonight's tracks.
No laptop and no player reads it: the database is gone for tonight, switch to the emergency flow below for the workaround route.
The permanent fix (at home)
Full-format the drive (not quick) as FAT32 + MBR. If the full format fails or crawls, retire the drive.
Re-export your library fresh from Rekordbox in Export mode. Never copy an old database back onto a clean drive.
Update player firmware and Rekordbox.
Carry two identical drives with identical exports. E-8302 on one drive is an inconvenience; on your only drive it's a disaster.
